# Self Attention

Use as the attention subskill in a carousel workflow, before visual production and again during review. Keep layout, assets, rendering, and export in the existing carousel skill when available. If that skill is absent, apply this companion independently and state that integration was not modified; do not invent a parent skill or an installation dependency.

Read [research and claim checks](references/research.md) when explaining platform mechanics, evaluating advice, or deciding whether evidence is stale. Read [examples and review](references/examples-and-review.md) when drafting or revising.

For Ela Maayan, use `el-hamaayan-engagement-lab` to choose the goal and own-post evidence, `el-hamaayan-social-templates` for verified Drive reference families, and `el-hamaayan-visual-design` for image choice, text placement, colors, and image-model briefs. Resolve companions by frontmatter name. Keep this skill focused on carousel entry points and sequence. For a small approved revision, apply only the relevant checks rather than reopening analytics or redesigning the whole post.

## 1. Set the attention brief

Infer these from the user's request; ask only for information that changes the work materially:

- Audience and situation: who should care, and what are they trying to do?
- Promise: what useful, interesting, funny, or moving thing will the carousel deliver?
- Main action: save, send, try a feature, visit a place, download, or contribute an idea.
- Proof available: genuine screenshots, photos, examples, quotes, and verified product behavior.

Treat attention as a sequence of viewer choices: notice the post, recognize relevance, choose to swipe, receive value, choose whether to continue or act. Use this as an editorial model, not an asserted Instagram ranking formula.

## 2. Design two entry points

Write slides 1 and 2 together. Give each a legible headline, enough context to stand alone, and an honest reason to continue. Offer two or three paired options during ideation; once copy is approved, preserve it unless asked to revise.

- Slide 1: identify a concrete problem, desire, surprise, or scene; make the promised payoff clear. Pair it with a relevant image that helps the viewer understand the point.
- Slide 2: introduce a different angle and begin delivering the promise. Name the subject again if needed for a viewer entering here. Use evidence, an example, a useful answer, or a sharper benefit rather than a duplicate cover.
- Read slide 2 in isolation: can a new viewer tell what this concerns, why it matters, and why to swipe next?
- Read 1 → 2 → 3: does the returning viewer gain something immediately rather than sit through two introductions?
- Avoid starting slide 2 with only a dependent phrase such as “And that's why,” “Because of this,” or “You asked, we delivered” without naming what was requested.

Ground this design choice in a qualified platform fact: Instagram may resurface a carousel starting on its second item. Do not say every carousel, each viewer, or the first two slides are guaranteed exactly two displays. Treat the second-hook recommendation as an editorial inference from the behavior, not an official ranking requirement.

## 3. Earn every next swipe

Create a compact slide map before designing:

| Slide | Job | Headline / visual | Value delivered now | Reason to continue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Use as many slides as the material earns. Do not prescribe a universal optimal length or pad a short idea. Start with the user's requested length when supplied.

- Deliver the first useful answer or proof early, usually on slide 2 or 3. Treat this as a default, not a platform rule.
- Give each slide one dominant idea; split content when the required reading becomes difficult on a phone.
- Alternate explanation with relevant visual evidence, examples, or changes in scale when it improves comprehension. Do not switch styles merely to create noise.
- Open a concrete question and answer it; make any next question arise from the answer. Avoid repeated teases or hiding all value until the last slide.
- Use real visual context: a bus stop and a destination can communicate access; a beautiful landscape alone may communicate only travel inspiration.
- Keep screenshots readable. Crop to the relevant proof, preserve its meaning, and identify mockups. Do not fabricate forum requests, user quotes, or feature behavior.
- Use the brand's visual system and voice. For Ela Maayan, write natural Israeli Hebrew, preserve RTL punctuation, and favor specific, friendly language over inflated “secret/hack” claims.
- End with a delivered payoff and one primary action connected to it. Use a genuine question when community input is the goal; avoid unrelated comment bait or several competing commands.

## 4. Review attention before export

Review two reading paths: 1 → 2 → rest, and 2 → rest. Check both text and actual phone-size visuals.

Flag missing context, repetitive openings, tiny proof, visual/text mismatches, broken promises, filler, and competing actions. Propose concrete replacement copy or a visual change for each important issue. Label any attention rating as an editorial judgment; never present it as predicted reach or measured retention.

Use music when it fits and the account's available rights and publishing tools support it. Explain that music can make a carousel eligible for Reels experiences, not guarantee placement or a boost. Check the actual interface before advising changes to an already published post. Do not issue a blanket instruction to retrofit every old carousel.

## 5. Learn from results honestly

Recommend a small, practical comparison with the account's similar organic carousels. Keep topic, posting age, audience size, distribution, and paid/collab status as comparable as possible; change one main variable, such as the second hook. Treat sequential posts as observational evidence, not a randomized A/B test.

- Record available metrics at consistent post ages, for example 24 hours and 7 days. Do not create a scheduled task unless requested.
- Separate reach (unique accounts) from views/impressions (repeat exposure may be included). Keep the provider's metric names and definitions.
- Track shares/reach and saves/reach where available, plus the chosen action. Call a metric sends/reach only when sends are actually exposed; do not relabel all shares as DMs.
- Use tracked visits or installs when available for conversion goals; mark attribution limits.
- Do not invent per-slide view counts, dwell time, swipe-through, completion, or drop-off from aggregate post metrics. Check whether the connected source actually exposes them; otherwise state that they are unavailable.
- Compare several matched posts and medians when useful. A rise in views alone does not establish better attention, second-slide performance, or new audience growth.

## Output

For creation, return the paired hooks, slide map, primary action, and brief reasons for consequential changes. For a quick review, return the largest attention issues and exact fixes. For research, distinguish confirmed statements, observational findings, editorial hypotheses, and unresolved claims; cite the relevant sources and date volatile claims. Keep the answer proportional to the request rather than printing this whole checklist.
