# El Hamaayan Brand Voice & Wordmark

## Purpose
Use this skill whenever writing:
- board titles
- rationale pages
- version notes
- short Hebrew or English brand lines
- wordmark direction notes
- future typeface / lettering ideas

This skill protects the tone of the identity work.

## Brand language principle
The writing should feel:
- human
- local
- smart
- simple
- honest
- a little rough in a good way
- rooted in the landscape and in real people

It should not feel:
- ad-agency polished
- startup-generic
- translated from English into awkward Hebrew
- spiritual in a vague way
- AI-sloppy or slogan-heavy

## Voice guidelines
Good voice traits:
- short
- grounded
- clear
- natural
- lived-in
- specific

Avoid:
- poetic fluff with no meaning
- fake depth
- generic “brand purpose” phrasing
- lines that sound like marketing exercises
- overclaiming

## Hebrew guidance
Hebrew should sound like a real Israeli human wrote it.
It can be thoughtful and crafted, but it should not sound sterile or translated.

Avoid phrases like:
- מקום של מקור
- generic source / essence language with no real texture
- fake-tourism brochure language

Prefer language that feels tied to:
- spring water
- routes and movement
- local discovery
- quiet places
- nature in Israel
- the way people actually talk about going somewhere

## English guidance
English copy should be direct and supportive, not tagline-driven unless the user explicitly asks for taglines.

Prefer:
- explanatory language
- concept naming
- short rationale
- simple decision notes

Avoid:
- polished mission-statement tone
- overdesigned branding clichés

## Wordmark principles
The handwritten feeling is a real strength.
Treat it as identity, not noise.

If refining the wordmark:
- refine structure and consistency
- protect the hand-drawn soul
- keep the Hebrew-local personality
- improve spacing, rhythm, and proportion
- avoid sanding it down into a generic font

## Future lettering direction
A future custom font or lettering system may grow out of this wordmark.
If discussing that future work, keep it framed as:
- a later expansion
- rooted in the existing handwritten DNA
- useful for headlines, social, merch, and identity assets

Do not jump into a full font project unless the user asks.

## Board-copy rules
When writing on concept boards:
- keep titles short and clean
- name the idea clearly
- explain the meaning plainly
- avoid slogan-like filler
- support the visual direction instead of competing with it

Examples of good board-writing directions:
- The spring within the landscape
- Route through the land
- Hidden source
- Path to water
- Mark shaped by terrain

## Rationale-writing rules
A rationale page should usually answer:
- what is the core idea
- what is strong about it
- what is weak about it
- what changed in this version
- what should happen next

Keep it readable and concise.

## Final rule
The language must help the identity feel more real, not more “branded.” The job is clarity and character, not advertising.
