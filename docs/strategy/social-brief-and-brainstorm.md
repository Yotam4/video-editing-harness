# Social brief and brainstorming workflow

Checked: 2026-11-10. Operational synthesis based on the [brainstorming source review](../research/brainstorming-methods.md), our brand guidance and the owner's requested modular content system. The workflow is a design recommendation, not an Instagram ranking rule.

## Start with the intended result

Before selecting a template, identify what the viewer should receive: useful information, recognition, enjoyment, an emotional connection, understanding, credible proof or a useful next action. A measurable campaign outcome can help, but not every creative idea needs a conversion goal.

Use existing context first. Read the current Git brief and handoff, the latest explicit user changes, brand and voice guidance, relevant own-post evidence, current product facts and available source assets. Check an operational Notion item for status when one exists; do not make Notion the only knowledge copy.

Record what is known, what is inferred and what is unresolved. If a consequential unknown remains, ask one short question that explains which choice it affects. If the task is clear, state a reasonable assumption and continue within the authorization already given.

## Choose the depth of the session

| Situation | Work required | What to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small approved revision | Read the existing brief and change request; apply it; run relevant checks | Restarting analytics, taxonomy or creative direction |
| New piece using an established type | Write a compact intent/asset brief; compare meaningful options if needed; make a reviewable draft | A long interview when the outcome and constraints are already clear |
| Unclear concept or requested brainstorming | Clarify the consequential uncertainty; explore distinct concepts; recommend one with tradeoffs | Choosing a polished format before understanding the job |
| New campaign, new reusable type or unfamiliar production method | Research the missing knowledge; prototype the risky part; document the proposed module and its dependencies | Creating a permanent skill from one vague idea, or forcing the entire campaign into one post |

## Build the brief

Keep it proportional. A clear repeatable item may need a few lines; a campaign needs a fuller record.

| Field | What it captures |
| --- | --- |
| Audience situation | Who is seeing this, what they know, and what makes this relevant now |
| Intended outcome | The useful, emotional or behavioral result; distinguish it from an optional CTA |
| Core promise | What the piece actually delivers, with no exaggerated tease |
| Evidence and proof | Verified facts, real product behavior, genuine story, demonstrations or dated results |
| Available assets | Stable Drive source IDs/URLs, real screenshots, footage and approved brand references |
| Constraints | Locked copy, people/places, rights, deadlines, production effort, language/RTL and facts that cannot be changed |
| Creative boundaries | What would make this feel wrong, misleading or unlike us |
| Classification | Format, subtype candidate, content intent, audience stage and campaign; do not collapse them into one label |
| Proposed next action | Include only if it follows naturally from the content; “none” is a valid choice |
| Measurement | A relevant available signal, observation window and attribution limit; enjoyment can also need qualitative feedback |
| Decision and handoff | Chosen approach, why, what was not chosen, what is approved, open questions and next step |

## Use funnel thinking as context

Audience stages describe a possible relationship, not the viewer's known personal history. Organic posts can reach mixed audiences, and one post may serve more than one stage.

| Audience situation | What often needs emphasis | Possible treatment | Optional action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery / TOFU | Immediate relevance, recognition, enjoyment or a useful standalone payoff | A real situation, a striking place with context, a joke, a helpful mini-guide | Share, save or none |
| Consideration / MOFU | How it works, a specific use case, trust, fit or an objection | A real walkthrough, comparison, founder explanation or community example | Explore, ask, try or none |
| Decision / BOFU | Concrete value, verified proof, practical next steps and important limitations | A current feature demo, genuine offer, result or task completion | Use the feature, install, RSVP or another actual action |
| Existing-user value / retention | Repeated usefulness, participation, belonging or a reason to return | A relevant update, contribution prompt, useful community story or advanced how-to | Contribute, revisit, report or none |

These are planning recommendations, not established rankings or automatic style rules. TOFU does not require a loud hook, MOFU does not require a carousel, and BOFU does not permit invented urgency. Choose the smallest explanation that serves this viewer and this piece.

## Generate genuinely different concepts

Develop several alternatives before polishing. For each serious candidate, identify its premise, visible subject, evidence mechanism, narrative progression, proposed format, effort and main weakness. Present two or three when the choice would help the user; recommend one when they have delegated the execution choice.

A useful concept comparison for “help people understand our public-transport feature” could be:

- **Real trip:** a person starts without a car, chooses a destination and shows the actual navigation choice. Requires current route information and suitable footage.
- **Product demonstration:** show the relevant app screen and real navigation options. Requires a genuine current screen recording or screenshot; this teaches the control directly.
- **Community origin:** show the real request and what changed, while making the forum itself understandable. Requires authorized, genuine source material and readable forum context.

These differ in proof and story, not merely in headline wording. They are illustrative options, not claims that the feature or particular source material was reverified during this methods review.

Vary the visual subject, narrative angle and voice when the user asks for variants. Do not produce the same voice and layout with synonyms and call that exploration.

## Converge and route

Select the concept that fits the intended result, proof, assets, production effort and brand. A lightweight storyboard, slide map or animatic can test the main uncertainty before a full render. Do not label a rough prototype as a finished export.

Classify across separate dimensions. An infographic is a presentation treatment that may be a carousel or video; a commercial is an intent that may use several formats. A funnel stage changes emphasis and proof requirements, not the permanent type automatically.

If the type already exists, reuse it and change its content. If it is ambiguous, show the plausible matches and the production difference. If it appears new, record a candidate with its distinctive input, output, process and review needs. Promote it into the registry when the distinction is concrete and useful; add a specialized skill only if there is a recurring procedure the generic workflow cannot express clearly.

Lock the accepted copy and meaningful constraints. Continue with the relevant production skill under the existing authorization. Do not introduce mandatory software spec-approval rounds for every reversible creative draft. Keep the established publishing boundary separate from creative preparation.

## Review and learn

Check the promise and payoff, actual visuals, phone readability, true Hebrew RTL, proof integrity and any CTA. For carousels, inspect both opening-slide entry paths. For videos, review the actual timeline, motion, screen legibility, captions and sound-dependent information. A branded frame or transcript alone is not visual QA.

Save the brief, concepts considered, decision, approved wording and handoff in Git. Save media and exports in Drive, using stable references in the Git record. Commit knowledge before refreshing its mirror beneath the Ela Maayan Hub. Record experimental recommendations as experiments and measure the signal actually available; do not invent slide-level retention or downstream attribution.

## Thin skill responsibilities

The `el-hamaayan-social-brainstorm` skill routes the session through this document rather than reproduce its research. Its procedure can be brief: read context; size the task; establish outcome/proof/constraints; resolve only consequential uncertainty; compare distinct concepts; classify or record a candidate; write the chosen brief; hand off to production; save the rationale and next step.

A separate routing skill can classify the content and load a subtype module. A production skill owns execution and artifact QA. An evidence-review skill owns observations and experiments. A knowledge-maintenance skill owns Git updates and Notion mirroring. Avoid one giant skill that tries to own all four jobs.
